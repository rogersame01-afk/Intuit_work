Synthesis

With corrected consent flags (GDC and MUC are not mutually exclusive — 65–70% consent to both), the updated picture:

  • Both GDC+MUC (65.7% of treatment): This dominant group is soft at S2C ITC 99.3 with flat RPn (99.9). The consent experience adds slight friction for users who go through both consent flows.
  • GDC Yes, MUC No (4.2% of treatment): The smallest group but the most concerning — S2C ITC 96.3 and RPn ITC 94.6. Users who consent to GDC but decline MUC are converting at a significantly lower rate in treatment.
  • GDC No, MUC Yes (9.1% of treatment): Positive signal at S2C ITC 100.7 and RPn ITC 102.2. This group grew from 7.6% → 9.1%, suggesting some users who previously consented to GDC are now only consenting to MUC.
  • Neither (21.0% of treatment): Strong positive at S2C ITC 106.3 and RPn ITC 106.0. This group grew from 18.9% → 21.0%. Users who decline both consents in treatment convert at much higher rates than control non-consenters — likely because the marginal users pushed into "neither" by treatment friction are higher-intent users who would have consented and completed in control.
  • Net result: The "Both" group (66% of treatment) being soft at 99.3 dominates the aggregate. The "Neither" group's strong ITC of 106.3 partially offsets but is only 21% of population. The "GDC Yes, MUC No" group at ITC 96.3 is a red flag but only 4% of users.

Caveat: This is a post-treatment analysis. Users who consent vs. don't are self-selected — the S2C differences reflect user characteristics, not a causal effect of consenting. The causal interpretation is about how the treatment experience changes who ends up in each bucket.

10 Root Cause Summary

  • Overall read: Soft (Conversion ITC 99.7, RPV ITC 99.9)
  • Main drivers:
    • DIY Free is the standout cohort at ITC 98.8 (-0.79pp), driving the majority of the aggregate gap on a 99K user base (24.6% of population). This cohort has the lowest tolerance for friction.
    • Mobile App users show ITC 99.3 (-0.44pp on 65K users). The consent UI may be more intrusive on the app surface.
  • Supporting evidence:
    • Abandonment shifts at Personal Info (+0.09pp) and Wages & Income (+0.09pp) — stages where consent likely appears
    • Total interactions down -0.54 per user (ITC 98.8) — users disengage earlier
    • FnF reach rate down marginally (ITC 99.8) — consistent with early-funnel dropout
    • S2I and I2C both soft at ITC 99.8 — drag is distributed, not concentrated
    • No mix shift in any dimension — effect is purely within-cohort
  • Hypotheses:
    • Likely driver: The §7216 consent experience introduces friction in the early-to-mid funnel, causing a small fraction of marginal users — especially DIY Free and Mobile App — to abandon before completing.
    • Supporting signal: The interaction count drop (ITC 98.8) confirms users are disengaging earlier, not struggling past a single gate.
    • Open question: The Day 6 ITC dip to 98.5 could indicate worsening as later-entering users have different urgency profiles, or simply weekend volatility.
  • Confidence: Medium — Signal is directionally consistent across dimensions, but magnitude is small and experiment has only 7 days of data.

11 Suggested Follow-Ups

  • Monitor Day 6+ ITC — if the weekend dip to 98.5 persists into weekday data, the effect may be larger than the aggregate suggests
  • DIY Free deep dive — cut by milestone within DIY Free to pinpoint exactly where the consent flow creates friction
  • Mobile App UX review — the -0.44pp suggests the consent UI may need platform-specific optimization
  • Consent interaction rate — measure what % of treatment users actually encounter the consent prompt and what % dismiss/accept
  • WIP analysis — check if the ~647 "lost" completes are truly lost or just delayed (still in progress)
  • PMT heterogeneity — run PMT read to identify subgroups with significantly stronger or weaker treatment effects
