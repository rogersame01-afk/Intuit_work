Key Findings

1. Pricing Is the Universal Pain Point

It's the #1 friction type in both PRS (128K, 71% high) and MPS (44K, 55% high) and dominates the entire top 12 on Reddit. OOP is almost entirely refund/billing demands. Only callchat and community — where customers are actively trying to file — don't surface pricing. This suggests pricing frustration is real, broad-based, and not just a vocal minority.

2. Calculation Errors Are a Mid-Flow Trust Threat

Tax calculation errors appear in MPS (14K, 100% high severity), callchat, and community — but are nearly absent from PRS (621). This means refund accuracy concern is overwhelmingly a mid-flow signal: users worry their refund looks wrong while still preparing. By the time they reach PRS (post-filing), the concern has either been resolved or has caused them to abandon.

3. PRS and MPS Tell Different Stories

PRS (post-filing, 76% positive) surfaces pricing and UI frustration from completers. MPS (mid-flow) uniquely surfaces lack of confidence (18K) and support resolution failures (16K) — signals invisible to PRS because they occur before filing. Neither survey alone gives the full picture; splitting them reveals distinct failure modes at different stages.

4. Reddit Is a Leading Indicator of Brand Risk

Reddit is 57.4% negative with public calls to "switch to competitor," "warn others," and "discontinue service." These are not resolvable with support — they require product and pricing changes. Reddit sentiment is a leading indicator of churn risk.

5. Community Reveals Self-Service Gaps

Community is where customers go when the product fails silently — calculation errors, import failures, missing features. 0.1% positive sentiment means almost nobody comes to community with good news. Fixing the top community friction types would reduce support contact volume.

6. OOP Is the Last Resort — 95% Negative

Out-of-product contacts are almost uniformly refund/billing disputes. These are customers who have exhausted in-product and support channels. The signal here is not "what's wrong" but "how bad does it get" when other channels fail.

Pricing: PRS + MPS + Reddit + OOP

Pricing surfaces independently in PRS (#1 at 128K, 71% high severity), MPS (#1 at 44K, 55% high severity), Reddit (top 8 of 12 friction types are pricing-related), and OOP (refund demands for overcharges). PRS and MPS both flag pricing, but PRS is 2.5x the rate — post-filing users are more vocal about price than mid-flow users.

Calculation Errors: MPS + Callchat + Community (not PRS)

Tax calculation accuracy is the #4 theme in MPS (14K, 100% high severity) but is nearly absent from PRS (only 621 records). It also surfaces in callchat (form validation errors, 76% high severity) and community (#1 friction type, 90% high severity). The key insight: refund calculation concern is a mid-flow signal (MPS) — users worry their refund looks wrong while still preparing, not after filing.

MPS-Only Signals: Confidence + Support Failures

"Lack of confidence" (18K) and "unresolved support issue" (16K) appear exclusively in MPS. These are mid-flow signals from users who chose "Not So Good" or "Don't Ask" when the product checked in. They don't surface in PRS (post-filing) because customers who lacked confidence mid-flow either resolved the issue or abandoned before getting a PRS survey.

The answer: it's overwhelmingly "too expensive," not "surprised by the price"

~131K friction points (89%) are pure value perception — customers know what they're paying and think it's too much. Only ~8K (5%) cite transparency issues (hidden fees, unexpected charges, misleading pricing). Another ~8K (5%) complain about upselling fatigue. This suggests transparency improvements have landed — the "free to paid" surprise is now a small minority of the signal. The dominant complaint is sticker shock on a price they saw clearly.

Four Distinct Failure Modes

Each source reveals a different facet of the same problem. Callchat shows form validation blocking users from filing (W2 box 15, state forms, spouse data). MPS captures mid-flow users who worry their refund looks wrong before they've finished entering data — a confidence signal, not necessarily a bug. PRS confirms that some calculation concern persists post-filing (5.5K), though at much lower volume. Community surfaces specific, reproducible calculation bugs (senior deduction, SSA, inherited IRA, doubling entries). Together they paint a picture ranging from blocking errors to confidence gaps to silent inaccuracies.

The dominant signal is "Am I doing this right?" — not "Is TurboTax broken?"

The largest bucket (53K, 55%) is general uncertainty — users mid-flow who aren't sure they've entered things correctly. Only 17% of these are high severity. The "general uncertainty" is addressable with better in-flow reassurance; the "trust" and "accuracy" concerns require demonstrating correctness.

Three failure modes — and the scariest one is "wrong answers" at 84% high severity

The volume leader is unresolved issues (54K) — customers who simply didn't get their problem fixed. But the highest-severity category is incorrect support answers (6K, 84% high severity) — agents providing wrong information that could lead to filing errors. Poor support quality (51K, 76% high severity) and support access / disconnection (10K) round out a picture of a support experience that's failing on quality, accuracy, and reliability. Disconnections alone account for 10K friction points — customers who got cut off and never reconnected.
