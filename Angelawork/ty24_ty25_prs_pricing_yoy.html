Apples-to-Apples Comparison · Same 17 sub-types as the Cross-Source Analysis

Survey bases: TY24 = 3.50M responses, TY25 = 3.41M responses.

Classification method: This comparison uses the exact same 17 friction sub-type names from the original TY25 VoC Cross-Source Analysis — 5 for "Too Expensive," 7 for "Surprise / Transparency," and 5 for "Upselling Fatigue." No fuzzy matching. This ensures the YoY comparison measures the same signal, not a wider net. The PRS deep-dive used broader LIKE-based matching and found larger totals (205K value, 61K transparency) because it captured the long tail of related sub-types.

Headline: The Conclusion Holds

"Too Expensive" still dominates — 89% of classified pricing friction in both years

Using the same 17 sub-types: TY24 had 205K value friction points (93% of total). TY25 had 195K (89%). The original cross-source finding that pricing friction is overwhelmingly a value perception problem, not a transparency problem, is confirmed and stable across years. The transparency share grew from 3% to 5% — a real increase, but from a very small base.

The mix shifted at the margins — value still overwhelmingly dominates

Value went from 93% to 89%. Transparency grew from 3.1% to 5.0% (+1.9pp). Upselling grew from 3.9% to 5.8% (+1.8pp). The shift is real but modest — "Too Expensive" is still nearly 18x larger than transparency using this classification. The growth in transparency and upselling reduced value's share, but value's absolute volume barely changed.

Severity and sentiment shifts: likely a calibration artifact

Every category shows severity up and negative sentiment down. This systematic pattern suggests an LLM extraction calibration change between TY24 and TY25 processing runs, not a true behavioral shift. Within-year relative differences (transparency is more severe than value) remain the reliable signal. Cross-year absolute severity levels should be compared cautiously.

So What

The original conclusion holds: "Too Expensive" overwhelmingly dominates

Using the same classification, value friction is 89% of the total in TY25 (down from 93% in TY24 but still nearly 18x transparency). The core finding from the cross-source analysis — that pricing friction is a value perception problem, not an information problem — is stable year over year.

But transparency is the fastest-growing edge

+59% volume (+63% rate-normalized). Every one of the 7 transparency sub-types grew. The two biggest movers are disclosure failures ("unclear pricing disclosure" +107%, "pricing transparency issue" +87%). At 5% share, transparency isn't the main story — but it's the margin that's moving in the wrong direction, and it's the one with the clearest product fix.

Why the deep-dive found bigger transparency numbers

The PRS deep-dive used broad LIKE-based matching and found 61K transparency friction points (19% of total) vs. 10.8K (5%) here. The difference is the long tail: sub-types like hidden fees (576), unexpected billing charge (1,342), pricing confusion (636), unclear pricing communication (564), and dozens of others that the original 7-name list didn't include. Neither is wrong — the narrow list is more conservative and comparable; the broad list captures the full extent of transparency-related friction. Both tell the same directional story: transparency is small relative to value, but growing fast.
