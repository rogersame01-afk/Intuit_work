Campaigns ranged from massive (CSO-861, PY E-file Rejects: 1.4M users) to small (CSO-870, BizTax Rejects: 45 users). All campaigns were identified by outreach_type IN ('Service_Recovery', 'Service_Recovery_-_Money').

Approach 1: Propensity Score Matching (All 46 CSOs)

Method

Coarsened Exact Matching (CEM) — creates exact strata on binned covariates, then compares treated vs. control within each stratum. 5:1 control-to-treated ratio, 1,000 bootstrap iterations for 95% confidence intervals.

  • Treated: 1,485,217 SR email recipients (filtered to users with PAM auth records)
  • Control: 7,425,694 matched non-SR users from product_analytics_master
  • Covariates: TTO segment, start SKU bin, prior-year completion flag
  • Match coverage: 100%

Selection bias warning: The negative ATT does not mean SR campaigns hurt conversion. SR recipients were targeted precisely because they experienced product issues (e-file rejects, import bugs, snag screens). These issues depress completion regardless of the email. CEM on segment/SKU/PY behavior cannot control for the underlying product trigger itself.

Approach 2: Randomized Holdout Analysis (8 CSOs)

Method

8 of the 46 CSOs had built-in holdout groups — users randomized into an email arm (treated) vs. a webhook-only arm (no email delivered). Both groups experienced the same product issue; the only difference is whether they received the recovery email. This is the clean causal design.

  • Treated: 967,405 email recipients
  • Control: 42,705 holdout users
  • 45,613 users appearing in both arms were removed
  • 2,000 bootstrap iterations for 95% CIs

These imbalances explain why the pooled estimate (-7.4 pp, statistically significant) is misleading — it is driven by composition differences across CSOs, not by the email treatment. The per-CSO estimates, which compare within the same randomized population, are the correct lens.

Key Findings

  1. SR emails do not drive incremental completes or revenue. Within the randomized holdout design, affected users complete at roughly the same rate regardless of whether they receive the recovery email.
  2. The naive PSM comparison is heavily confounded. Matching on observable covariates alone produces a -10.6 pp ATT that reflects selection bias (treatment triggered by the product issue), not the causal effect of the email.
  3. The holdout design is the right lens. By comparing within the same randomized, affected population, it isolates the email's true effect — which is negligible for conversion and revenue.
  4. SR emails may still have value beyond conversion. Customer satisfaction (PRS/NPS), reduced contact volume, and next-year retention were not measured in this analysis and could be meaningful benefits of the outreach.

Methodology Notes

Approach 1 — Coarsened Exact Matching

  • Individual-level treated population built from tax_src.src_braze_turbotax_email_send joined to canvas names from the SR aggregate table
  • Control pool: all TY25 product_analytics_master users not in treated set (~60M)
  • Revenue from monetization_analytics_master
  • Matching strata: segment bin × SKU bin × PY completion flag (48 strata)
  • 617,509 treated users with no PAM auth record excluded (received email but never authed)

Approach 2 — Randomized Holdout

  • Treated arm identified via src_braze_turbotax_email_send; holdout arm via src_braze_turbotax_webhook_send (same canvas names)
  • 45,613 users appearing in both arms removed to prevent contamination
  • Users with no PAM record excluded (551K treated, 19K holdout)
  • Bootstrap CIs: 2,000 iterations, seed=42, percentile method
  • 4 of 8 holdout CSOs skipped due to insufficient sample size (<10 in one arm)
